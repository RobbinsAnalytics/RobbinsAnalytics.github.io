to the first accepted solution.

Aaron Robbins brings an established delivery practice into the team's approved environment. First establish what exists, what is permitted and what must be ready. Then open a reliable route from a business question to an accepted result.

  • Scope before commitment: begin with one meaningful business area and the people accountable for it.
  • Human entry and exit: the requester confirms the need and accepts or rejects the delivered solution.

01

Agree where to begin.

The first working days establish a bounded company context, rather than assuming that every tool or dataset is ready for use.

The company brings

  • A sponsor and a first business area.
  • Decision owners, domain knowledge and existing work.
  • Approved access paths, policies and constraints.

Aaron establishes

  • The first scope, audience and desired outcome.
  • Where company sources, decisions and evidence belong.
  • Who resolves gaps and authorizes new uses.

First checkpoint: a named scope, accountable people and a path to the sources and rules needed for assessment. The first scope may be a team or domain; it need not be the entire enterprise.

02

Prepare the operating foundation.

Foundation Readiness proceeds through Inventory → Rationalize → Operationalize. A first bounded scope may take roughly 20–45 days to prepare; actual timing is set after the first inventory and depends on access, complexity and company decisions.

  1. 01

    First scoped assessment

    Inventory what exists and what is permitted.

    Map reports, data, definitions, owners, dependencies, standards, approved technology and AI platforms within the declared scope. Record access and documentation gaps.

    Output: a scope map with known assets, gaps, decision owners and permitted-use boundaries.

  2. Inventory gateRequests can be captured.

    The queue retains the requester's words and shows captured, waiting for intake. No work is approved or scheduled yet.

  3. 02

    After inventory

    Rationalize the choices.

    Resolve competing versions and definitions, identify authoritative sources and justified variants, and decide which company rules apply to the first scope.

    Output: selected sources, standards, exceptions and unresolved decisions with named owners.

  5. 03

    Before formal intake

    Operationalize the selected foundation.

    Verify access, usable interfaces, applicable checks, human review, maintenance and recovery. Configure the approved tools and document what this scope can support.

    Output: a usable operating boundary and an evidence-backed readiness decision.

  6. Readiness gateFormal intake opens.

    Queued requests return to their owners for the full intake questions. Execution begins only after a request passes those gates.

Inside the inventory and readiness work

Learn the rules before changing the tools.

Document what is approved, what remains uncertain and who can decide. Existing company standards take precedence over personal defaults.

Engineering environment

  • Approved stack: languages, frameworks, runtimes and repositories.
  • Build requirements: coding, review, testing and security.
  • Deployment paths, support ownership and exceptions.

AI work envelope

  • Approved platforms, models, accounts and integrations.
  • Permitted data: classes, connections and actions.
  • Oversight: logging, retention, evaluation and human review.

04

Open one accountable request route.

A request can be small. The route still confirms the business question, checks what exists and keeps a human decision at entry and exit.

Two human decisions frame each delivery.

Delivery lead: confirms the applicable process, tests, evidence and recorded limits for the version under review.

Requester: accepts, asks for correction or rejects the business result, with reasons. Additional company approvers apply where required.

The requester completes a human retrospective before release. Approved release, operating ownership and future changes follow the company's procedures.

05

Leave a stronger route for the next request.

The first application delivers a result for a real decision and establishes what can be reused, maintained or changed in the next one.

  • An accepted solution.Business meaning, sources, checks and limits stay with the reviewed version.
  • An operating owner.Access, refresh, change and recovery responsibilities are assigned where the solution needs them.
  • Learning with a disposition.Human feedback informs adoption, later work or a recorded decision.
