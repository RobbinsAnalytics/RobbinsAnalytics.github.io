03 / The questions

Understand their work.
Find where to help.

After introductions and some context about their role, the frame is simple: these questions are asked with data and reporting in mind.

  1. What are your biggest challenges right now, and where does data make them harder?

  2. Where could better data or reporting make the biggest difference?

  3. What would make my role a success for you at 90 days?

  4. One or two questions tailored to the person, for example:

    • Staff engineer: When building or troubleshooting a feature, what data helps your team make decisions, and what is difficult to get or trust?
    • Operations manager: What information helps your team spot ride issues early, and where do you still have to rely on manual follow-up?

  5. If you were me, what would you focus on?

  6. Who else should I be talking to?

04 / How the learning works

Listen, reflect back, and check understanding.

Learn the people and the work

  • Get to know people: understand their work and learn where I can be useful.
  • Learn through conversation: focus on roles, workflows, and experiences.
  • No access needed: discuss the work without system access, company datasets, or patient information.

Make the understanding easy to check

  • Reflect back: send each person a short, private recap so they can correct anything I misunderstood.
  • Make it concrete: where useful, follow up with a small illustrative example using synthetic data.
  • Learn and validate: these examples help check understanding, without committing to operational delivery before joining.
