01 / Business problem

The business problem

It’s Friday morning. By Monday’s operations review, the delivery manager needs to know whether shipments are far enough behind plan to move people onto packing. Two reports give two different answers. One counts completed orders. The other counts units that actually shipped. Which number should drive the decision?

What disagrees

The delivery manager sees two shipment totals. One report counts completed orders. The other counts confirmed units shipped, including partial orders.

What Operations must decide

At Monday’s review, Operations needs to compare confirmed units with plan through the same date and decide whether to discuss shifting packing capacity.

What Friday’s dashboard must show

Actuals, plan and freshness through the agreed Thursday, June 18 cutoff. Friday handoff gives the designated leads time to verify access before the review.

The shipment scenario, including its dates, figures and records, is fictional.

Case and Product

The Case holds the request, commitment, released version and acceptance. The Product holds the approved version and, where applicable, run rules and support ownership. The case can close while the product stays in service.

Configuration and adoption identify local authorities, tools and record locations. Knowledge management reviews lessons for future work.

Worked example notes

Reuse decision and Friday commitment

The register suggests Monthly Order Completion. The requester declines it because completed orders miss units already shipped on partial orders. Planning checks existing commitments, assigns a delivery owner, and records the Friday snapshot in SHIP-042. Daily refresh remains open.

Approved inputs and what counts as done

The brief uses Confirmed Shipment Lines, the certified Confirmed Units Shipped metric, the approved plan through the same cutoff, and a visible Data through date. Acceptance criteria describe what leads need to do. The definition of done specifies reconciliation, shipment-line tests, access checks and versioned evidence.

What the records preserve

The Case holds the release evidence and acceptance decision. The Product links the released dashboard to its approved inputs and later service rules.

Worked example notes

Verification and recheck

Candidate v0.1 counts a 240-unit line twice when a join repeats it across status events. Quality records the blocker in SHIP-042. Delivery corrects the join and Quality rechecks v0.2.

Requester learning and release

The requester reviews v0.2 privately. The partial-shipment observation becomes a reviewed reuse checklist entry. Quality separately retains the duplicate-event test. Release authority approves the exact build released as v1.0.

First run and recovery

Service and the product owner record the daily refresh window, stale-data rule and support ownership in SHIP-VIEW. The June 23 run succeeds. On June 24, Service displays the last verified figures with a stale notice, reruns and reconciles when the late feed arrives, then records recovery. Service shares the incident lesson and opens SHIP-043 for better alerting.

Standards impact

A later Quality standard requires every expected site feed before a result can be marked current. A proposed register scan flags SHIP-VIEW. The business owner confirms continued need, Quality confirms applicability and records what can continue, and Demand receives SHIP-044. Both follow-up requests remain open.

05 / Scope

Scope and open questions

COS is a reusable operating model for analytics delivery and service. Each organization defines how the model fits its roles, systems, and governing requirements. The shipment example illustrates one possible implementation, including a proposed way to identify products affected by a standards change.

  • Which roles and decisions must every COS instance name, especially for release authority, business acceptance and standing service?
  • What minimum evidence belongs in every Case and Product record, and what can an instance reference from existing systems?
  • Which tools and controls can vary by instance, and what evidence shows that local choices meet the model’s requirements?

The operating method

The case can close.
Ownership continues.

Keep the evidence connected. Link the released solution to the business need and customer acceptance.

Own ongoing use. Give recurring service an owner, run rules and a path for support and change.

Carry the method forward. The operating method can travel as the team’s tools and organization change.
